\begin{abstract}
We apply hierarchical agglomerative clustering (HAC) on travel-time
distances -- mathematically equivalent to a percolation sweep over the
network's edges -- to the Roman road network, and ask whether the
clusters that emerge at different travel-time thresholds recover the
Roman provinces. We first validate the method on the exact network and
travel-time model used in a recent large-scale reconstruction of the
Roman road system, recovering the provincial partition with an Adjusted
Rand Index (ARI) of 0.70 (bootstrap 95\% CI [0.68, 0.71]) at a threshold
of $\sim$77 hours ($\sim$3.2 days) of travel. We then apply the same
method to our own independently cleaned network under a simpler,
constant-speed travel-time model, where the provincial alignment is
weaker (ARI = 0.43, 95\% CI [0.43, 0.45]) and peaks at a shorter
travel-time scale ($\sim$25--32 hours). Together these results indicate
that administrative provincial boundaries of the Roman Empire left a
recoverable, threshold-dependent signature in the connectivity structure
of its road network, and that the strength of that signature is
sensitive to the travel-time model used to weight the network.
\end{abstract}

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Data}

\paragraph{Road network.}
The primary network is built from the raw Itiner-e linework
\citep{desoto2025itinere}. Near-miss junctions and duplicate endpoints
are snapped, the linework is noded, and degree-2 chains are simplified,
giving a weighted graph of 8{,}460 nodes and 12{,}849 edges across 12
connected components (see \texttt{data\_cleaning/} in the accompanying
repository). Edge weights are travel times computed from edge length
at a constant walking speed of 2~km/h.

\paragraph{Validation network.}
To check that our results are not an artefact of our own cleaning
pipeline or travel-time model, we separately replicate the analysis on
the exact Itiner-e export and endpoint-noded topology used by
\citet{pazout2026network} (10{,}506 nodes, 14{,}770 edges, 13 connected
components, including ferry links across the Bosporus and the
Dardanelles), with travel time computed from Tobler's hiking function
\citep{tobler1993hiking} applied to slope and geodesic edge length. This
self-contained replication is kept in
\texttt{results/HAC/paper\_replication/} and does not otherwise modify
the primary analysis.

\paragraph{Province boundaries.}
Both networks are overlaid with the Ancient World Mapping Center's
``Roman Empire AD 200'' province polygons, corrected by A.\ Pa\v{z}out
\citep{awmc2023provinces} (57 provinces). Each edge is assigned to the
province containing its midpoint, falling back to the province with the
longest geometric overlap when the midpoint falls outside every
polygon; nodes inherit the province of their incident within-province
edges. Edges that cross a provincial boundary, or that cannot be
assigned to any province, are excluded from the alignment scoring
described below.

\subsection{Hierarchical agglomerative clustering and percolation}

Following the general approach of \citet{arcaute2016cities}, who use a
percolation sweep over distance thresholds between street intersections
to recover the hierarchical regional and urban structure of Britain, we
apply an equivalent percolation sweep to travel time on the Roman road
network itself, rather than to Euclidean distance between unconnected
points.

Within each connected component, we compute pairwise travel-time
distances between nodes and perform hierarchical agglomerative
clustering (HAC) using single linkage. Because single linkage merges
clusters at the weight of the minimum edge connecting them, cutting the
resulting dendrogram at a travel-time threshold $t$ reproduces exactly
the connected components of the network with all edges of weight
greater than $t$ removed -- i.e.\ HAC under single linkage is
mathematically equivalent to a percolation sweep over the network's
edges, computed once per component rather than independently at every
threshold.

Sweeping $t$ across the full range of merge heights gives, at each
threshold: the number of clusters, and the size of the largest cluster
relative to the network (the percolation curve). We additionally fit a
power law to the percolation curve over its emergent region.

\paragraph{Alignment with provinces.}
At each threshold $t$, we compare the resulting cluster labels to the
province labels of the nodes with a known province assignment, using
the Adjusted Rand Index \citep[ARI;][]{hubert1985comparing} and
Normalized Mutual Information \citep[NMI;][]{danon2005comparing}. ARI
ranges from 0 (random partition) to 1 (perfect agreement, up to cluster
relabelling); NMI ranges from 0 to 1 and does not penalize a finer
partition as harshly as ARI. We search exhaustively over every distinct
merge height for the threshold that maximizes ARI against the
provinces, and estimate a 95\% confidence interval on that best ARI (and
on the threshold it occurs at) by bootstrap resampling nodes with
replacement and re-selecting, for each resample, the best-scoring
threshold among those closest to the full-data optimum.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Validation on the manuscript's network (first result)}
\label{sec:validation}

Figure~\ref{fig:paper_replication} shows the percolation curve and
snapshot cluster maps for the validation network of
\citet{pazout2026network}, with Tobler-function travel times and AWMC
provinces (10{,}430 of 10{,}506 nodes have a known province).

Table~\ref{tab:validation} reports ARI and NMI at six representative
travel-time snapshots. Alignment with the provinces rises from
essentially zero at 6--12 hours, peaks around 1--3 days, and falls off
again as clusters grow beyond provincial scale.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Province alignment at fixed travel-time snapshots, validation
  network (\citet{pazout2026network}, Tobler-function travel time;
  $n=10{,}430$ nodes with known province).}
\label{tab:validation}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
Snapshot & Clusters & ARI & NMI \\
\midrule
6 h   & 2{,}809 & 0.039 & 0.640 \\
12 h  & 1{,}376 & 0.092 & 0.683 \\
24 h  & 514     & 0.201 & 0.734 \\
2 d   & 154     & 0.516 & 0.803 \\
7 d   & 22      & 0.493 & 0.778 \\
12 d  & 15      & 0.308 & 0.689 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

An exhaustive search over every merge height finds a single, sharp peak
at $t = 77.1$ hours ($\sim$3.2 days), with ARI $= 0.695$ (95\% bootstrap
CI $[0.681, 0.710]$). The peak is stable: every bootstrap resample of
the node set selects the same 77-hour merge height as optimal. At this
threshold the network's emergent regions correspond closely to the
Roman provincial map.

\subsection{Percolation structure of the itinereX network (primary result)}
\label{sec:primary}

Figure~\ref{fig:primary} shows the same analysis applied to our own
independently cleaned network, with constant-speed (2~km/h) travel
times (8{,}411 of 8{,}452 clustered nodes have a known province, across
56 represented provinces).

Table~\ref{tab:primary} reports the same six snapshots. As in
Section~\ref{sec:validation}, alignment is weak at sub-day thresholds
and improves markedly by 24 hours, but here it falls away again by 2
days rather than continuing to improve through the 1--3 day range.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Province alignment at fixed travel-time snapshots, primary
  itinereX network (constant 2~km/h travel time; $n=8{,}411$ nodes with
  known province).}
\label{tab:primary}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
Snapshot & Clusters & ARI & NMI \\
\midrule
6 h   & 4{,}455 & 0.029 & 0.615 \\
12 h  & 2{,}464 & 0.135 & 0.666 \\
24 h  & 652     & 0.418 & 0.753 \\
2 d   & 76      & 0.048 & 0.395 \\
7 d   & 8       & 0.018 & 0.209 \\
12 d  & 7       & 0.017 & 0.199 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

An exhaustive search over every merge height finds the best alignment at
$t = 31.8$ hours, with ARI $= 0.433$ (NMI $= 0.763$, 291 clusters),
95\% bootstrap CI on ARI of $[0.426, 0.448]$. Unlike the single sharp
peak found in Section~\ref{sec:validation}, the alignment curve here has
two nearby local maxima of comparable height, around 25 and 32 hours;
bootstrap resamples select between them, giving a wider 95\% CI on the
optimal threshold of $[24.8, 31.8]$ hours. The best achievable alignment
with the provinces (ARI $\approx 0.43$) is substantially weaker than the
$\approx 0.70$ recovered on the validation network of
Section~\ref{sec:validation}.
